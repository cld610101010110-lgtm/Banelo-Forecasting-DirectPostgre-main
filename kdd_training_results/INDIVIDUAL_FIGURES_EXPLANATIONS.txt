FIGURE: Decision Tree Training Process
FILE: Decision_Tree_Training_Process.png
-------------------------------------------------------------------

EXPLANATION: The Decision Tree training shows rapid initial learning, converging at iteration 9 with a final validation MAE of 1.63 units. However, the large gap between training error (~0.80) and validation error (1.63) indicates overfitting—the model memorizes training patterns rather than learning generalizable relationships. This is why Decision Tree performed worst among all models in the comparison.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
FIGURE: Random Forest Training Process
FILE: Random_Forest_Training_Process.png
-------------------------------------------------------------------

EXPLANATION: Random Forest demonstrates smooth, gradual improvement over 42 iterations, achieving a final validation MAE of 1.28 units. The ensemble approach of combining multiple decision trees reduces overfitting compared to single Decision Tree. The steady convergence shows consistent learning, though it requires more iterations than Linear Regression while achieving comparable accuracy.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
FIGURE: Gradient Boosting Training Process
FILE: Gradient_Boosting_Training_Process.png
-------------------------------------------------------------------

EXPLANATION: Gradient Boosting shows sequential learning behavior, with each iteration building upon previous models' errors. It converged at iteration 35 with a final validation MAE of 1.35 units. While more accurate than Decision Tree and Random Forest, it requires moderate training time and careful tuning to prevent overfitting. The gradual error reduction demonstrates the boosting mechanism effectively correcting mistakes.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
FIGURE: XGBoost Training Process
FILE: XGBoost_Training_Process.png
-------------------------------------------------------------------

EXPLANATION: XGBoost exhibits efficient learning with fast initial error reduction, converging at iteration 30 with a final validation MAE of 1.13 units. As an optimized gradient boosting implementation, it achieves the second-best accuracy among all models tested. The rapid convergence and small training-validation gap demonstrate good generalization, though Linear Regression still outperforms it in both accuracy and training speed.
