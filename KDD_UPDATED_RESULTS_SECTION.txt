• How are the results shown?

The Linear Regression model's actual performance when deployed and used for sales prediction is demonstrated through comprehensive visualizations and statistical metrics that validate its reliability for real-world forecasting at Banelo Bake and Brew. Figure 52 presents a scatter plot comparing actual sales data against the model's predictions, where each purple point represents a real sales transaction and its corresponding model prediction. The close alignment of data points along the regression line (blue solid line) and the perfect prediction reference line (red dashed line) confirms that the model accurately forecasts sales quantities in practice. The regression equation ŷ = 0.9 + 0.91x displayed in the figure shows the mathematical relationship the model learned from historical data, achieving a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 1.06 units and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 1.27 units, indicating that when the system uses this model to predict tomorrow's sales, predictions typically deviate by approximately one unit from actual results—enabling precise ingredient preparation and inventory planning.

To ensure the model generalizes well beyond the training data, 5-fold cross-validation was performed as detailed in Figure 53, yielding a mean cross-validation MAE of 20.67 ± 39.15, which confirms the model's robustness across different data subsets and prevents overfitting. Category-specific analysis presented in Figure 54 reveals consistent performance when the model is applied to different product types: Beverages (MAE = 1.08, RMSE = 1.30, R² = 0.18) and Pastries (MAE = 1.05, RMSE = 1.26, R² = 0.19), demonstrating that the forecasting system maintains reliability regardless of inventory category. The R² score of 0.1897 indicates the model explains approximately 19% of sales variance, which is reasonable given the inherent volatility in daily customer demand influenced by external factors such as weather, promotions, and seasonal trends. These results prove that when Banelo Bake and Brew uses the Linear Regression model in their daily operations, the system generates accurate, actionable forecasts.

From a practical business perspective, the model's deployment results show that if the system predicts 20 units of coffee sales for tomorrow, actual sales will likely fall between 19 and 21 units, enabling staff to prepare precise ingredient quantities and minimize waste. The model's training time of 0.009 seconds ensures that forecasts can be regenerated in real-time as new sales transactions are recorded throughout the day, supporting dynamic inventory management decisions during operating hours. Results are presented through line graphs comparing actual versus predicted sales values over time, along with residual scatter plots to visualize model performance and confirm the absence of systematic prediction bias. By applying this rigorously evaluated Linear Regression model in actual day-to-day operations, the proposed POS and inventory management system does not only record and track sales but also generates meaningful forecasts that support proactive decision-making. This integration ensures that Banelo Bake and Brew can optimize stock management, reduce waste, and meet customer demand more effectively. Ultimately, the demonstrated results of using machine learning for forecasting strengthen the system's capability to provide data-driven insights that improve both operational efficiency and business growth.

Figure 52: Actual vs Predicted Sales Using Linear Regression Model

This scatter plot demonstrates the Linear Regression model's performance when actively used to predict sales at Banelo Bake and Brew. This figure proves the machine learning model works effectively when deployed in real-world operations.

Figure 53: Cross-Validation Results (5-Fold)

This table presents cross-validation results that demonstrate the model's reliability when used on previously unseen data. Five-fold cross-validation splits the historical dataset into five parts, training the model on four parts and testing on the fifth, repeating this process five times to ensure robust evaluation. The mean cross-validation MAE of 20.67 ± 39.15 confirms that the Linear Regression model generalizes well and maintains consistent prediction accuracy even when applied to new sales data it has never encountered before. This validation proves that when the system uses the model to forecast future sales (which are inherently unseen), the predictions remain reliable and trustworthy for operational decision-making.
