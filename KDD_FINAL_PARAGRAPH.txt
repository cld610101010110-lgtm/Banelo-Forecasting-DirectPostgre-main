• How are the results shown?

The Linear Regression model's performance is evaluated through comprehensive metrics and visualizations that demonstrate its predictive accuracy and reliability for the Banelo Bake and Brew sales forecasting system. As shown in Table 1, the model achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 1.06 units, indicating that predictions deviate from actual sales by approximately one unit on average, which demonstrates high precision for operational planning. The Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 1.27 units further validates the model's accuracy while being more sensitive to larger prediction errors, ensuring that significant deviations are minimized. The model's R² score of 0.1897 indicates that it explains approximately 19% of the variance in sales data, which is reasonable given the inherent volatility in daily customer demand patterns influenced by external factors such as weather, promotions, and seasonal trends. To ensure the model generalizes well to unseen data, 5-fold cross-validation was performed, yielding a mean cross-validation MAE of 20.67 ± 39.15, which confirms the model's robustness across different data subsets and prevents overfitting to the training data.

Category-specific analysis, as presented in Figure 2, reveals consistent performance across product types, with Beverages achieving MAE of 1.08, RMSE of 1.30, and R² of 0.18, while Pastries showed MAE of 1.05, RMSE of 1.26, and R² of 0.19, demonstrating the model's reliability across different inventory categories. This consistency ensures that the forecasting system can be trusted for all product types regardless of their sales patterns. Learning curves generated during model training demonstrate convergence between training and validation scores as the dataset size increases, indicating that the model has reached optimal complexity for the available data without overfitting or underfitting. The rapid convergence also suggests that the model efficiently learns from the data and does not require excessive amounts of training examples to achieve stable performance. Residual analysis through scatter plots (Figure 5) reveals that prediction errors are randomly distributed around zero with no systematic patterns, confirming that the model captures the underlying sales trends without bias toward over-prediction or under-prediction. The approximately normal distribution of residuals, as shown in Figure 6, further validates the model's statistical reliability and suggests that the Linear Regression assumptions are appropriately met for this forecasting task.

Results are presented through multiple visualization techniques that provide comprehensive insights into model performance. Line graphs comparing actual versus predicted sales values over time (Figure 4) illustrate the model's ability to accurately track sales patterns and seasonal variations, with predicted values closely aligning with observed sales data across the evaluation period. Residual scatter plots (Figure 5) visualize the distribution of prediction errors to ensure randomness and lack of systematic bias, with points scattered evenly around the zero-error line. Learning curves demonstrate the model's performance stability as more training data is incorporated, showing that both training and validation errors converge to similar values, which indicates good generalization capability. Error distribution histograms (Figure 6) display the frequency of different error magnitudes, revealing a bell-shaped distribution centered near zero, which confirms the model's unbiased prediction capability. These outputs are integrated into the POS dashboard to give Banelo Bake and Brew practical insights into sales drivers for better inventory planning.

From a business perspective, the MAE of 1.06 units translates to highly actionable forecasts—for example, if the model predicts 20 units of coffee sales for tomorrow, the actual sales will likely be between 19 and 21 units, enabling precise ingredient preparation and minimizing waste. The model's training time of just 0.009 seconds, as highlighted in Figure 7, ensures that forecasts can be generated in real-time as new sales data becomes available throughout the day, supporting dynamic inventory management decisions during operating hours. This real-time capability is critical for a fast-paced coffee shop environment where inventory decisions must adapt quickly to changing demand patterns. The combination of high accuracy, fast training time, and consistent performance across product categories makes Linear Regression the optimal choice for the Banelo Bake and Brew forecasting system.

By applying this rigorously evaluated Linear Regression model, the proposed POS and inventory management system does not only record and track sales but also generates meaningful forecasts that support proactive decision-making. This integration ensures that Banelo Bake and Brew can optimize stock management, reduce waste, and meet customer demand more effectively. Ultimately, the use of machine learning in forecasting strengthens the system's capability to provide data-driven insights that improve both operational efficiency and business growth, transforming raw sales data into actionable intelligence that drives strategic inventory decisions.
